# Captioning the Capital: Multimodal Topic Modeling and the National Photo Company Collection

**Author:** Nabeel Siddiqui, Susquehanna University

[![Binder](https://mybinder.org/badge_logo.svg)](https://mybinder.org/v2/gh/jdh-observer/KQ9eLAJzKXNb/main?filepath=article.ipynb)

## Abstract

This article uses vision-language models and neural topic modeling to analyse 35,368 digitalised photographs from the National Photo Company Collection (1909-1932), housed at the Library of Congress. By generating natural language captions with Florence-2 and clustering them with BERTopic, the study identifies 527 distinct subject categories dominated by government offices and military formations. A nearly four-to-one ratio of male to female references in the captions quantifies the collection's systematic marginalisation of women. The 23.7 percent of images that resist topical classification (the noise cluster) reveals both the limits of automated analysis and unexpected archival heterogeneity. These findings suggest that commercial photography operated as a "visual infrastructure of legitimation," systematically reinforcing governmental authority through standardised compositional conventions.

## Data Availability

The analysis data is included in this repository:

- **captions.csv**: 35,368 image captions generated by Florence-2
- **topic_model_results.csv**: Topic assignments for each image
- **topics.json**: Topic keywords and metadata

The original images are available from:
- **Library of Congress**: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/npco/
- **Wikimedia Commons**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:National_Photo_Company_Collection

## Reproducibility

The notebook follows a "show pipeline, load results" pattern:
- Heavy computation code (captioning, topic modeling) is shown but commented out
- Pre-computed results are loaded from CSV files for immediate analysis
- Researchers can uncomment and run the full pipeline with appropriate hardware
